# OEP-0000 — Foundational Inquiry

**Status:** Draft  
**Type:** Foundational  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Date:** 2026-09-08

---

## 1. Abstract

ORBIT is an evolving, tool-agnostic methodology for collaboration
between humans and AI agents.

ORBIT is not intended to be a programming framework, a prompt
library, an agent configuration, or a collection of instructions
for a specific AI tool.

Its purpose is to investigate and define how humans, AI agents,
knowledge, capabilities, workflows and tools can collaborate
effectively to build and evolve software projects.

ORBIT itself is being designed through the same principles that
it intends to establish.

This document is therefore not a final specification.

It is the first foundational inquiry into what ORBIT is, what
problem it attempts to solve, and which concepts may constitute
its foundations.

---

# 2. The Problem

The emergence of increasingly capable AI agents is changing the
nature of software development.

The problem is no longer simply:

> How can we make an AI write code?

The deeper question is:

> How can humans and AI agents collaborate effectively to achieve
> a shared intention within a project?

An agent may have significant reasoning capabilities and access
to large amounts of general knowledge, yet still produce an
incorrect result.

It may:

- misunderstand the desired outcome;
- use irrelevant context;
- apply an inappropriate workflow;
- use the wrong tool;
- misunderstand project conventions;
- violate project constraints;
- modify something outside the intended scope;
- produce a technically valid but functionally incorrect result.

Therefore, ORBIT investigates how intention, knowledge, context,
capabilities, agents, workflows, tools and verification interact
within a collaborative system.

---

# 3. Vision

ORBIT aims to become a general methodology for agentic
collaboration.

It should be applicable to:

- new projects;
- existing projects;
- frontend projects;
- backend projects;
- full-stack applications;
- design systems;
- SaaS products;
- personal projects;
- teams;
- research and learning environments.

ORBIT should remain independent from any particular AI model,
vendor, IDE or development tool.

Tools such as Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini or future systems
may implement or consume ORBIT concepts, but they should not
define ORBIT itself.

---

# 4. What ORBIT Is Not

ORBIT is not:

### 4.1 An AI Agent

ORBIT does not attempt to create or replace an AI model or agent.

### 4.2 A Prompt Library

Prompts may be useful within ORBIT, but prompts are an interaction
mechanism, not the methodology itself.

### 4.3 A Claude Code Configuration

Claude Code may be one implementation of ORBIT, but ORBIT must
remain conceptually independent from it.

### 4.4 A Collection of Markdown Files

Documentation is one possible representation of knowledge.

The documentation itself is not ORBIT.

### 4.5 A Rigid Recipe

ORBIT should provide structure without forcing every project to
have the same implementation.

### 4.6 A Fixed Methodology

ORBIT must be capable of evolving as AI capabilities, tools,
projects and human-AI collaboration evolve.

---

# 5. Foundational Hypothesis

ORBIT begins with the following hypothesis:

> The quality of collaboration between humans and AI agents depends
> not only on the capabilities of the AI, but also on how intention,
> knowledge, context, capabilities, tools, workflows and
> verification are structured around a task.

This is a hypothesis.

It must be continuously challenged and validated.

ORBIT must not turn assumptions into principles merely because
they appear intuitive or convincing.

---

# 6. Intention

## 6.1 Candidate Classification

**Provisional Foundation**

ORBIT currently considers Intention to be a foundational concept.

The current definition is:

> **Intention is the explicit representation of what is desired
> to be achieved, why it is desired, and, when relevant, the
> constraints under which it should be achieved.**

Intention provides direction for the collaboration.

Without a sufficiently clear intention, different participants
may optimize for different outcomes.

This applies to:

- humans;
- AI agents;
- workflows;
- tools;
- automated processes.

---

## 6.2 Shared Intention

ORBIT currently proposes:

> **Collaboration begins with a shared intention.**

The purpose of "shared" is important.

The goal is not necessarily that every participant possesses the
same internal reasoning.

The goal is that the participants have a sufficiently aligned
understanding of the desired outcome.

---

## 6.3 Provisional Principle

> **Intention before execution.**

Execution should not be treated as the starting point of a
collaborative task.

A sufficiently clear intention should guide the subsequent
selection of knowledge, context, capabilities, workflow and tools.

This principle remains open to revision.

---

# 7. Knowledge

## 7.1 Candidate Classification

**Provisional Foundation**

ORBIT currently considers project knowledge to be a foundational
resource.

However, "knowledge" must not be interpreted as all possible
knowledge possessed by an AI or human.

The relevant knowledge is primarily the knowledge required to
understand and operate within a particular project and task.

---

## 7.2 Project Knowledge

Project knowledge may include:

- domain knowledge;
- architecture;
- technical conventions;
- business rules;
- design decisions;
- historical decisions;
- constraints;
- implementation patterns;
- project-specific procedures;
- accumulated experience.

ORBIT does not attempt to reproduce all general knowledge
already available to an AI model.

Instead, it focuses on the knowledge required to collaborate
effectively within a specific project.

---

# 8. Knowledge and Context

ORBIT currently distinguishes between Knowledge and Context.

### Knowledge

The knowledge available within the project ecosystem.

### Context

The subset of knowledge, state, instructions and constraints
relevant to a specific task.

Conceptually:

    KNOWLEDGE
        |
        | selection / transformation
        v
      CONTEXT
        |
        | applied to
        v
       TASK

Therefore:

> **Context is currently considered a transformation or selection
> of knowledge rather than an independent foundation.**

---

# 9. Context

## 9.1 Candidate Classification

**Derived Mechanism — Provisional**

Context is task-dependent.

A developer working on a form may require one context.

The same developer working on a table may require another.

A project may therefore contain a large body of knowledge while
a particular task requires only a small subset of it.

ORBIT therefore investigates the concept of:

> **Minimum Sufficient Context**

The objective is not to provide the maximum amount of information
to an agent.

The objective is to provide enough relevant information for the
agent to make appropriate decisions for the task.

---

# 10. Skills

## 10.1 Candidate Classification

**Derived Capability — Provisional**

ORBIT currently does not consider Skills to be a fundamental
requirement.

A human or AI agent can perform a task without a formally defined
Skill.

Skills are instead considered a possible way to encapsulate
reusable knowledge, procedures and experience.

Conceptually:

    KNOWLEDGE
        +
    EXPERIENCE
        +
    PROCEDURE
        |
        v
      SKILL

Skills may improve consistency, reuse and efficiency, but they
are not currently considered necessary for collaboration to exist.

This classification remains open to revision.

---

# 11. Agents

## 11.1 Candidate Classification

**Open**

ORBIT has not yet determined whether Agents constitute a
fundamental concept, a participant, a mechanism or another
category.

The existence of increasingly autonomous agents creates questions
about:

- autonomy;
- responsibility;
- specialization;
- coordination;
- human oversight;
- decision-making.

These questions remain open.

---

# 12. Workflows

## 12.1 Candidate Classification

**Open**

A workflow may describe how a task progresses through multiple
steps.

For example:

    UNDERSTAND
        |
        v
      PLAN
        |
        v
     EXECUTE
        |
        v
     VERIFY
        |
        v
      LEARN

However, ORBIT has not yet established whether workflows are:

- fundamental;
- derived;
- procedural;
- optional;
- or emergent from other concepts.

---

# 13. Tools

## 13.1 Candidate Classification

**Open**

Tools provide capabilities for taking actions.

Examples include:

- reading files;
- modifying files;
- executing commands;
- querying APIs;
- interacting with external systems.

ORBIT currently distinguishes capability from intention.

Having access to more tools does not necessarily produce better
results.

The relationship between Tools, Agents, Skills and Workflows
remains under investigation.

---

# 14. Verification

## 14.1 Candidate Classification

**Open**

ORBIT currently proposes the concept:

> **Verification before trust.**

An agent's statement that a task has been completed should not
automatically be considered evidence that the result is correct.

Whenever possible, results should be evaluated through observable
evidence such as:

- tests;
- builds;
- static analysis;
- functional validation;
- review;
- measurable outcomes;
- other appropriate verification mechanisms.

The appropriate level of verification should depend on the risk
and impact of the task.

Whether Verification is a foundation, a mechanism, a workflow
stage, or a transversal property remains open.

---

# 15. Evolution

## 15.1 Candidate Classification

**Foundational Property — Provisional**

ORBIT must be capable of evolving.

AI models evolve.

AI capabilities evolve.

Tools evolve.

Software projects evolve.

Human-AI collaboration evolves.

Therefore, a methodology designed to remain static would
eventually become obsolete.

ORBIT should not define evolution as constant change.

Instead:

> **Evolution is the capability to deliberately change the
> methodology when evidence, experience or technological change
> provides sufficient reason to do so.**

ORBIT itself must therefore contain a mechanism for evolving its
own principles.

---

# 16. OEP — Orbit Evolution Proposals

ORBIT will evolve through **Orbit Evolution Proposals (OEPs)**.

An OEP is a formal proposal for introducing, modifying,
questioning, rejecting or removing an idea from ORBIT.

An OEP should preserve not only the final decision but also the
reasoning that produced it.

Conceptually:

    IDEA
      |
      v
    PROPOSAL
      |
      v
    DISCUSSION
      |
      v
    CRITIQUE
      |
      v
    EXPERIMENTATION
      |
      v
    EVALUATION
      |
      +-------> REJECTED
      |
      +-------> MODIFIED
      |
      v
    ACCEPTED

---

# 17. ORBIT Must Challenge Itself

No ORBIT principle should be accepted merely because:

- it sounds reasonable;
- it is intuitive;
- an expert proposed it;
- an AI suggested it;
- the majority agrees with it.

Important concepts should be treated as hypotheses until they
have survived meaningful criticism and, when possible, practical
experimentation.

A principle should be considered stronger when alternative
explanations or counterexamples fail to invalidate it.

---

# 18. Facts, Hypotheses and Proposals

ORBIT should explicitly distinguish between:

### FACT

Something supported by reliable evidence.

### HYPOTHESIS

An idea that appears plausible but has not yet been sufficiently
validated.

### PROPOSAL

An idea currently being considered for inclusion in ORBIT.

### PRINCIPLE

A proposal that has been sufficiently debated, evaluated and
accepted into the methodology.

This distinction is important because ORBIT must avoid turning
uncertainty into false certainty.

---

# 19. Collaboration Between Intelligences

ORBIT is not designed under the assumption that humans will
always possess more knowledge than AI agents.

Future AI systems may possess knowledge and capabilities far
beyond those of individual humans.

Therefore, ORBIT should not be fundamentally based on the idea
that humans must transfer all relevant knowledge to an AI.

Instead, ORBIT investigates a broader question:

> **How can different intelligences collaborate effectively when
> they possess different levels and types of knowledge and
> capability?**

The methodology should remain useful whether the AI has:

- less knowledge than the human;
- comparable knowledge;
- substantially more knowledge;
- or capabilities that humans cannot directly reproduce.

---

# 20. Alignment

A central hypothesis emerging from the foundational discussion is
that future human-AI collaboration may depend increasingly on
alignment rather than simply knowledge transfer.

The relevant alignment may include:

- intention;
- goals;
- constraints;
- context;
- expected outcomes;
- responsibilities;
- verification;
- boundaries.

This concept is not yet accepted as an ORBIT foundation.

It is an important area for future investigation.

---

# 21. Current Conceptual Model

At the current stage, ORBIT does not assume that the candidate
concepts form a simple hierarchy.

The current model is instead treated as a conceptual system whose
relationships must be discovered.

Current candidates:

    INTENTION
    KNOWLEDGE
    CONTEXT
    SKILLS
    AGENTS
    WORKFLOWS
    TOOLS
    VERIFICATION
    EVOLUTION

Current provisional classifications:

    INTENTION
        -> Foundation

    KNOWLEDGE
        -> Foundation

    CONTEXT
        -> Derived mechanism

    SKILLS
        -> Derived capability

    AGENTS
        -> Open

    WORKFLOWS
        -> Open

    TOOLS
        -> Open

    VERIFICATION
        -> Open

    EVOLUTION
        -> Foundational property, provisional

These classifications are hypotheses and may change.

---

# 22. Current Decision

ORBIT currently accepts provisionally:

### Foundation

- Intention
- Knowledge

### Derived

- Context
- Skills

### Open

- Agents
- Workflows
- Tools
- Verification

### Foundational Property

- Evolution

None of these classifications should be considered permanently
established.

---

# 23. Open Questions

The following questions remain open for future OEPs:

### Q1

Is Intention truly a fundamental element of collaboration?

### Q2

Is Knowledge a foundation, or is it a resource derived from an
even more fundamental concept?

### Q3

Is Context fundamentally a transformation of Knowledge?

### Q4

Are Skills merely encapsulated knowledge and procedure, or do
they represent a distinct capability?

### Q5

What is the fundamental role of an Agent within ORBIT?

### Q6

Are Workflows fundamental structures or simply expressions of
Intention and Capability?

### Q7

Are Tools merely capability providers?

### Q8

Is Verification a stage, a mechanism, or a transversal property?

### Q9

Is Evolution a principle, a property, or the mechanism by which
all other principles remain valid over time?

### Q10

Are we missing a more fundamental concept such as Alignment,
Governance, Agency or another concept?

### Q11

What is the minimum set of concepts required to describe
effective human-AI collaboration?

### Q12

Can ORBIT remain general enough to survive technological change
without becoming too abstract to be useful?

---

# 24. Scope of OEP-0000

This OEP does not define:

- repository architecture;
- directory structures;
- Skills implementation;
- Agent implementation;
- Workflow implementation;
- prompt templates;
- Claude Code configuration;
- Codex configuration;
- specific development practices;
- frontend or backend architecture;
- technology choices.

Those decisions must emerge from future investigation and OEPs.

---

# 25. Success Criteria

ORBIT should not be evaluated by the number of documents,
prompts, Skills, Agents or workflows it contains.

Its success should ultimately be measured by whether it improves
collaboration between humans and AI agents.

A successful implementation of ORBIT should make it possible to
answer positively to questions such as:

- Does the agent better understand the intended outcome?
- Is relevant context easier to identify?
- Is irrelevant context reduced?
- Are results more predictable?
- Are errors easier to detect?
- Can results be verified?
- Can decisions be understood after the fact?
- Can the methodology adapt to new AI capabilities?
- Can the methodology survive changes in tools and models?
- Can the methodology be applied to both new and existing
  projects?

---

# 26. Meta-Principle

ORBIT must use its own methodology to develop itself.

Therefore:

> **ORBIT must be capable of questioning, testing and evolving
> ORBIT itself.**

This creates a recursive property at the heart of the project:

    ORBIT
      |
      v
    defines a methodology
      |
      v
    the methodology defines how it evolves
      |
      v
    ORBIT uses that process to evolve itself

This property is itself subject to future evaluation.

---

# 27. Status

**DRAFT**

OEP-0000 is not considered accepted.

Its purpose is to establish the initial conceptual territory of
ORBIT and record the reasoning from which future proposals will
emerge.

The next stage is to critically evaluate the current conceptual
model and determine whether the proposed foundations and
classifications are correct.

No conclusion in this document should be considered immutable.
